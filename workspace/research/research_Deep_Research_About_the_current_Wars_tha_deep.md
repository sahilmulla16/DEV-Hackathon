## Introduction

The Middle East remains one of the most strategically volatile regions in the world, with multiple overlapping conflicts contributing to regional instability and global ripple effects. Since October 2023, the Israel-Hamas war has reignited longstanding tensions in the Levant, triggering humanitarian crises, regional escalation risks, and renewed geopolitical competition. Concurrently, Houthi attacks on commercial vessels in the Red Sea have disrupted one of the world’s most critical maritime trade corridors. Meanwhile, broader power shifts—such as the China-brokered rapprochement between Iran and Saudi Arabia—and persistent instability in countries like Syria and Yemen continue to redefine alliance structures and security calculations worldwide.

These developments are not confined to the Middle East. They intersect with global supply chains, energy markets, defense strategies, and transnational security policies. As major powers like the United States and China vie for influence, regional actors are increasingly engaging in strategic hedging, diversifying partnerships beyond traditional Western alliances. Simultaneously, energy-importing nations in South and Southeast Asia face mounting economic pressures due to fluctuating oil prices, while East Asian manufacturing hubs confront logistical challenges from rerouted shipping lanes.

For instance, India’s export of the BrahMos supersonic cruise missile system to the Philippines serves as a counterbalance to China’s naval expansion in the South China Sea [Source: *Strategic Autonomy or Strategic Risk? - Robert Lansing Institute*]. As China strengthens its defense and energy partnerships in the Middle East, Indo-Pacific allies are likely to reassess their own strategic dependencies. The success of China’s mediation between Iran and Saudi Arabia demonstrates that Beijing can offer alternative security frameworks, which may encourage Indo-Pacific states to explore diversified alliance models.

This shift does not necessarily indicate a direct realignment against the U.S., but it does complicate U.S. strategic calculations by normalizing partnerships with China even among traditional American allies. In essence, **multipolarity is becoming entrenched across regions**, and U.S. influence is increasingly shared with emerging powers like China.
