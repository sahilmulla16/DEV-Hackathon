# Summary: Muslim and Hindu Population by Country or Region

This report provides a comprehensive overview of the global demographic distribution of Muslim and Hindu populations, focusing on absolute numbers, national proportions, regional concentrations, and key demographic trends over the past two decades. Drawing on authoritative sources such as Pew Research Center, Wikipedia, and international demographic analyses, the findings highlight the geographic and demographic centers of these two major world religions and examine shifts in religious composition within diverse, multi-faith nations.

---

## Key Points

- **Indonesia, Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, and Nigeria** are the top five countries by absolute number of Muslims, with Indonesia leading at approximately 231 million.
- Despite its Hindu majority, **India has the third-largest Muslim population globally**, exceeding 200 million—making it the largest Muslim population in a non-Muslim-majority country.
- **Muslims form the majority** in Indonesia, Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Nigeria (all over 85%), but constitute only about **14–15% of India’s population**.
- **India and Nepal** are the only two countries where Hindus form a majority, with 79% and 81% of their respective populations identifying as Hindu.
- **Approximately 95% of the world’s Hindus live in India**, and nearly **99% reside in the Asia-Pacific region**, underscoring the religion’s deep regional concentration.
- Over the past two decades, **Muslim populations have grown faster than Hindu populations** globally and in key countries like India and Indonesia, reflecting higher fertility rates and demographic momentum.
- While precise longitudinal data for the full 2000–2020 period is limited, trends suggest a **relative increase in the Muslim share** of national populations in religiously diverse countries such as India.

## Main Arguments / Findings

### 1. **Geographic Concentration of Muslim Populations**
The global Muslim population is heavily concentrated in South and Southeast Asia. Indonesia leads with an estimated 231 million Muslims, representing about **12% of the world’s total Muslim population** and making it the country with the largest Muslim community [Source: Muslim Population by Country 2026; IslamiCity]. Pakistan follows closely with around 212 million, and India ranks third with over 200 million Muslims—despite Islam being a minority religion there [Source: Islam by country - Wikipedia].

Bangladesh and Nigeria round out the top five, with approximately 153 million and 106 million Muslims, respectively. In all of these countries except India, Muslims constitute a strong majority—**over 85%**—of the national population [Source: Countries with the most Muslims & global Muslim population...]. This contrast highlights a key distinction between **absolute numbers** and **proportional representation**: India’s massive overall population allows it to host one of the largest Muslim communities in the world, even though Muslims remain a minority.

### 2. **Hinduism’s Demographic Core: India and Nepal**
Hinduism is one of the most geographically concentrated major world religions. Only **two nations—India and Nepal—have Hindu majorities**. In India, Hindus account for **79% of the population**, while in Nepal, they represent **81%** [Pew Research Center, 2025]. Together, these two countries are home to virtually all of the world’s Hindus.

India alone hosts approximately **1.1 billion Hindus**, which constitutes **about 95% of the global Hindu population** [Pew Research Center]. The broader Asia-Pacific region contains **nearly 99% of all Hindus worldwide**, with smaller communities in Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Indonesia, Malaysia, and Myanmar [Seasia.co, 2025]. Outside Asia, significant Hindu diaspora populations exist in the **United States, United Kingdom, Canada, and the Caribbean**, particularly in Trinidad and Tobago and Guyana, but these remain religious minorities without majority status.

### 3. **Demographic Trends Over the Last Two Decades**
From 2010 to 2020, **Islam was the fastest-growing major religion globally**, driven largely by higher fertility rates and younger population structures among Muslim communities [Pew Research Center]. This trend is evident even in religiously diverse countries like **India and Indonesia**.

In **India**, the Muslim population reached **213 million in 2020**, accounting for **15% of the national total** [Countries with the most Muslims & global Muslim population change, 2010...]. Although Hindus still form the overwhelming majority (79%), demographic analyses suggest a **relative decline in the Hindu share** of the population over time, with some sources describing a “quiet decline” amid the surge in Muslim population growth [organiser.org]. This shift, while gradual, has significant implications for social, political, and cultural dynamics in India’s pluralistic society.

In **Indonesia**, home to roughly **240 million Muslims**, the religion remains dominant, with Islam deeply embedded in national identity and governance [IslamiCity]. While detailed year-by-year demographic shifts are not fully documented in the provided sources, Indonesia continues to serve as a central pillar of the global Muslim community. No substantial evidence suggests a decline in Muslim proportion within Indonesia; rather, the country remains a stable Muslim-majority nation with sustained population growth.

These trends reflect broader patterns: **Muslim populations are expanding rapidly both in absolute terms and relative to other religious groups**, even in countries where they are not the majority. This growth is not limited to Muslim-majority nations but is also visible in large, secular democracies like India.

## Takeaways

1. **Population Size vs. Religious Majority**: High absolute numbers of religious adherents do not necessarily correlate with majority status. India exemplifies this: it has the **third-largest Muslim population in the world**, yet remains a **Hindu-majority country**.

2. **Regional Concentration**: Both Islam and Hinduism are highly concentrated regionally. **South Asia dominates both religious landscapes**, with India serving as a critical nexus—hosting the largest Hindu population and one of the largest Muslim populations.

3. **India’s Unique Position**: India stands out as a country of immense religious diversity and demographic significance. It is the **only nation with both a large Hindu majority and a very large Muslim minority**, making it a key case study in interfaith dynamics and demographic change.

4. **Faster Growth of Muslim Populations**: Over the past two decades, **Muslim populations have grown faster than Hindu populations**, both globally and within key countries like India. This is attributed to **higher fertility rates, younger age structures, and natural population increase**, rather than conversion or migration in most cases.

5. **Stability of Hindu Majority in Core Countries**: Despite demographic changes, **Hindu majorities remain stable in India and Nepal**, and there is no indication of a shift toward minority status in these core nations. However, the **relative size of Muslim communities is increasing**, which may influence future policy, education, and intergroup relations.

6. **Global Implications**: As the world’s second-largest religion, **Islam’s demographic expansion** has global implications for migration, urbanization, and religious pluralism. Meanwhile, **Hinduism remains largely regionally bound**, with limited growth outside South and Southeast Asia, though its diaspora continues to grow culturally and economically influential.
