This report synthesizes the most recent reliable demographic data on Muslim and Hindu populations globally, with a focus on key countries including India, Indonesia, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nigeria, and Nepal. Drawing from authoritative sources such as the Pew Research Center, Wikipedia, and regional demographic analyses, the report outlines absolute population sizes, national percentages, growth trends, and the socio-demographic implications of religious composition in these nations.

## Key Points

- **India** is the most populous country in the world (approx. 1.477 billion) and hosts the largest Hindu population and the largest Muslim population outside of Muslim-majority countries.
- Hindus constitute approximately **79–80%** of India’s population, while Muslims make up **14–15%**, with both communities growing in absolute numbers.
- **Indonesia** has the world’s largest Muslim population in absolute terms (over **242 million**), representing about **13% of the global Muslim population**.
- **Pakistan and Bangladesh** are Muslim-majority nations and key contributors to South Asia’s status as the region with the largest concentration of Muslims globally (**31% of the world’s Muslims**).
- **Nigeria** has the **fifth-largest Muslim population** globally, with Islam forming a plurality or majority, particularly in the north, and higher fertility rates contributing to its demographic growth.
- **Nepal**, historically a Hindu-majority nation, remains predominantly Hindu, but the proportion of Hindus has declined since 1950, reflecting increasing religious diversity following constitutional secularization.
- Across these countries, religious demographics are shaped by **fertility rates, historical legacies, migration, and state policies**, influencing social cohesion, political dynamics, and intergroup relations.

## Main Arguments / Findings

### 1. **India: A Pluralistic Giant with Growing Religious Communities**

India’s religious demography is defined by its vast population and deep religious diversity. As of the most recent estimates, India’s total population stands at approximately **1.477 billion**, surpassing China as the world’s most populous nation [India Population 2026]. While the last official census with detailed religious data was conducted in 2011, projections and analyses by the **Pew Research Center** provide reliable interim estimates.

Between the last two censuses, the Hindu population grew by **138 million (13.8 crore)** and the Muslim population by **34 million (3.4 crore)** [Pew Research, *Population growth and religious composition in India*]. This indicates that although the **growth rate** of the Muslim population has been higher, both communities are expanding significantly in absolute numbers due to India’s large demographic base.

Hindus are estimated to constitute **79–80%** of the population, while Muslims represent **14–15%**, making India home to the **largest Muslim population outside of Muslim-majority countries** [Wikipedia, *Religion in India*]. The Muslim community is predominantly **Sunni (85%)**, with **Shia Muslims** accounting for about **13%**, and smaller groups such as **Bohras, Khojas, Ahmadiyya, and Quranists** forming the remainder [Find Easy, *Muslim Population in India 2026*].

This demographic structure underscores India’s pluralistic character, though rising religious polarization in recent years has brought attention to the political and social implications of these demographic trends.

### 2. **Indonesia, Pakistan, and Bangladesh: Centers of Global Islam**

Indonesia, Pakistan, and Bangladesh are pivotal to the global Islamic demographic landscape, each representing different models of Muslim-majority societies.

- **Indonesia** has over **242 million Muslims**, the largest national Muslim population in the world, accounting for **13% of all Muslims globally** [Muslim Population by Country 2026]. While the exact percentage of Muslims in Indonesia is not specified in the sources, estimates from external research suggest it exceeds **87%**, with significant religious minorities including Christians, Hindus, and Buddhists. Indonesia’s dominance is **numerical rather than proportional**, meaning its significance lies in the sheer size of its Muslim population rather than near-universal adherence.

- **Pakistan and Bangladesh**, both in South Asia, are more homogeneously Muslim. South Asia as a region is home to **31% of the world’s Muslims**, the largest regional share [Wikipedia, *Islamic world*]. Although precise national percentages are not provided in the sources, both countries are constitutionally and culturally defined by Islam. Pakistan, founded as a homeland for South Asian Muslims, is estimated to be over **96% Muslim**, with the vast majority being Sunni. Bangladesh is similarly Muslim-majority, with Muslims comprising approximately **90%** of the population.

The contrast between these nations reveals a key demographic insight: **Indonesia leads in absolute numbers**, while **Pakistan and Bangladesh exhibit higher proportions** of Muslims within their populations. This reflects deeper institutional entrenchment of Islam in the latter two, where religion is closely tied to national identity and state structure.

Together, these three countries highlight **Asia’s centrality to global Islam**, with over **40% of the world’s Muslims** residing in South and Southeast Asia.

### 3. **Nigeria and Nepal: Contrasting Religious Landscapes in Pluralistic Societies**

#### Nigeria: Muslim Plurality and Regional Religious Divides

Nigeria has the **fifth-largest Muslim population** in the world, with Muslims forming a **plurality or majority** of the population [Wikipedia, *Religion in Nigeria*]. While exact percentages are not provided in the sources, external data (e.g., Pew Research) estimates that **roughly 50–53%** of Nigerians are Muslim, with the remainder predominantly Christian and adherents of traditional African religions.

A key demographic trend is the **higher fertility rate among Muslims** compared to Christians, which has led to a gradual increase in the Muslim share of the population and a relative decline in the Christian proportion [Wikipedia, *Religion in Nigeria*]. The majority of Nigerian Muslims are **Sunni**, often affiliated with **Sufi brotherhoods** such as the **Qadiriyya, Tijaniyyah, and Mouride orders**. There are also small communities of **Shia, Ahmadiyya, and Mahdiyya** Muslims.

Ethnically, the **Hausa** in the north are almost entirely Muslim, while the **Yoruba** are religiously mixed, practicing both Islam and Christianity alongside traditional beliefs. This religious geography has contributed to **deep regional divides**, with the **north being predominantly Muslim** and the **south more Christian**, influencing political alignments, governance, and intercommunal relations.

#### Nepal: Declining Hindu Majority in a Secularizing State

In Nepal, **Hinduism remains the majority religion**, but its share of the population has been **declining since 1950** [Share of Religious Minorities A Cross-Country Analysis (1950–2015), May 2024]. Historically, Nepal was the world’s only **Hindu kingdom**, but it was declared a **secular state** in 2008 following the abolition of the monarchy.

While the sources do not provide exact figures for the Hindu or Muslim populations, external data (e.g., Nepal’s 2011 census) indicates that **Hindus constituted about 81.3%** of the population, with **Muslims at around 4.4%**, primarily concentrated in the southern Terai region. The decline in the Hindu share—though still dominant—reflects both **lower fertility rates among Hindus**, **increased religious awareness among ethnic minorities**, and **constitutional recognition of religious freedom**.

Unlike Nigeria, where religion correlates strongly with **regional and ethnic divisions**, Nepal’s religious dynamics are more influenced by **constitutional change and demographic shifts** rather than interfaith conflict. The Muslim community, though small, is growing, and religious diversity is increasing, particularly among indigenous groups embracing Christianity or asserting non-Hindu identities.

## Takeaways

1. **India remains the epicenter of both Hindu and Muslim populations globally**, with over **1.17 billion Hindus** (80%) and **over 200 million Muslims** (14–15%)—the latter being the largest Muslim minority in the world. Both communities are growing in absolute terms, reinforcing India’s role as a key player in global religious demography.

2. **Asia dominates the global Muslim population**, with **Indonesia, Pakistan, and Bangladesh** collectively accounting for a massive share. While **Indonesia leads in absolute numbers**, **Pakistan and Bangladesh** reflect higher religious homogeneity, illustrating different models of Muslim-majority societies.

3. **Fertility rates significantly influence religious demography**, as seen in **Nigeria**, where higher Muslim fertility is shifting the religious balance, and in **India**, where differential growth rates have sparked political discourse despite overall growth in all communities.

4. **Constitutional and historical contexts shape religious landscapes**: 
   - In **Nigeria**, religion is intertwined with **ethnicity and regional identity**, contributing to political and social tensions.
   - In **Nepal**, the transition from a **Hindu kingdom to a secular republic** has accelerated religious diversification, even as Hinduism remains dominant.

5. **Religious demography is not static**—it evolves due to **fertility, migration, state policy, and identity assertion**. Accurate, up-to-date census data is critical for understanding these dynamics, yet gaps in recent data (e.g., India’s delayed 2021 census) pose challenges for researchers and policymakers.

6. **Global religious trends are increasingly shaped by South and Southeast Asia**, where the majority of Hindus and a large proportion of Muslims reside. These regions will continue to influence interfaith relations, political Islam, and global religious discourse in the 21st century.
